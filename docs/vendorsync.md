# Vendor ingestion (VendorSync) reference

Scope rules for who may edit what are in CLAUDE.md (Scope boundary, Ingestion phases). This file holds the rules and vendor table definitions.

## Ingestion rules (summary, verified against `config/vendorsync.php`)

- **Safety Jogger.** Scope: `articletype` in `article_types` (`shoes`, `workwear`, `gloves`, `head_protection`, `fall_protection`, `overshoes`, `accessories`); `excluded_brands` is empty. Only `shoes` also needs an EN ISO 20345 or 20347 norm (`SafetyJoggerScope`, `SafetyJoggerScope::FOOTWEAR_ARTICLE_TYPE`) — every other listed type is in scope on its own, because none of them ever carries either norm. `zero-protective` only for models `high_boot` and `high` with EN ISO 20345; `low_boot` stays standard-rated until the accountant confirms, and every non-footwear article type is standard rate always (`SafetyJoggerMapper::mapNonFootwear()`). Stock buffer 0, and the 15 minute stock feed is the stock authority. SKUs are identified by `skuId`, never by EAN. RRP includes VAT at 20%, for every article type. Category: `shoes` by norm family, model and `config('vendorsync.safety_jogger.categories')`, unchanged; `workwear` by `workwear_product_category` and `workwear_categories`; `head_protection` by `headprotection_product_subcategory` and `head_protection_categories`; `gloves`, `fall_protection` and `overshoes` each have exactly one destination (`simple_categories`); `accessories` by a name heuristic in the mapper (the vendor gives this type no categorisation field at all). An unresolved category (an unknown code, or a type the fixture does not model) drafts the product (`unmapped_category`), same as an unmapped Footsure `Group Class`; it does not stop it being created. A multi-unit SKU (`pieces` or `buyPer` not 1: a pack, a box, a dozen) is skipped and reported for every article type, not only shoes, pending the vendor confirming what such a price covers. A "Winterbox" bundle (a boot plus socks, gloves and a beanie sold under one SKU) is detected by the vendor's own name starting with `PROMO` and drafted (`DraftReason::BUNDLE`) rather than published as a single boot.
- **Footsure.** Imports everything except brands starting with `Safety Jogger` (case-insensitive), and a children's, boys' or girls' line, which is out of scope entirely (`FootsureMapper::isChildrens()`, reason `children`): a `Group Class` starting with `Childrens`, `Boys` or `Girls`, or, outside those, a product whose own name says `Junior`, `Jnr`, `Kids` or `Childrens`. A small adult size is not affected by this: only the `Group Class` or the product's own name decides it, never a size value. Brand `Miscellaneous Other` maps to no brand. Every `Group Class` seen in the feed is mapped to a leaf collection (`config('vendorsync.footsure.categories')`, about 150 entries); an unmapped one (a new Group Class the vendor introduces) drafts the product. A footwear leaf's own EN ISO rating can override which footwear leaf a product lands in (`FootsureMapper::footwearLeaf()`; see data-contract.md, Collections): this never moves a product in or out of footwear, only between its leaves. Outside the footwear leaves, a product is sized on the garment scale (`garment_size`, `App\Domain\Catalogue\Options\GarmentSizes`), standard rate always, with no safety class, features, standards, toecap, midsole, outsole, upper, lining or fastening written — none of it means anything on a jacket or a glove. The one exception is the `Insoles` Group Class, whose `UK Size` and `EU Size` columns are genuinely shoe sizes: it is sized as footwear (`shoe_size`) although its collection, `insoles-laces-shoe-care`, is not a footwear leaf. Stock buffer 1, stock comes from the daily catalogue run. Placeholder measurements (`999`, `333`, `222`, `111`) are stored as null. A feed file older than 36 hours aborts the run; one dated more than 60 minutes ahead is unreadable.
- **Run safety.** A run that sees fewer than 70% of the previous run's products aborts before writing. A product is drafted after 2 missed runs and never deleted. A run holds its lock for 4 hours. The schedule only registers when `VENDORSYNC_SCHEDULE_ENABLED=true`. Failed, aborted and stale runs mail `VENDORSYNC_ALERT_EMAIL`.
- **Price.** Listing price ex VAT = cost x markup, rounded half up to the penny, with no cap at the RRP. The markup is `VENDORSYNC_MARKUP` (default 1.5), overridable with `VENDORSYNC_SJ_MARKUP` and `VENDORSYNC_FS_MARKUP`; each must be a number from 1.0 to 5 or the run stops when the mapper is resolved. Each mapper has its own `PriceCalculator`. `compare_price` is the RRP ex VAT only when it is above the price. Safety Jogger `rrpPrice` is VAT-inclusive at 20%; Footsure `SRP` is treated as VAT-inclusive at the row's own rate (the feed does not say so: unconfirmed). A missing or zero RRP means no compare price and no check. No cost means the SKU is not imported.
- **Draft reasons for price data.** `cost_at_or_above_rrp`: every variant has a cost at or above its RRP ex VAT (probably a pack cost against a unit RRP). Those variants are held at stock 0, and one such variant among others does not draft the product. `non_gbp_price`: any SKU priced in a currency other than GBP, or with a price but no currency, that has no stored GBP price (a first import, a new SKU, a variant already unpriced). Such SKUs are imported unpriced (no price row, no cost) with stock 0, never converted, and their product stays draft even when its images arrive. A SKU that does have a stored GBP price is held instead (see Currency instability below). The image pipeline only clears `no_image`; every other stored reason keeps a product draft. Runs report `non_gbp_variants`, `non_gbp_products` (only variants actually left unpriced, not held ones) and a `non_gbp_currency` tally (`(blank)` apart from an explicit code such as `EUR`), dry runs included. The fix for a `non_gbp_price` product is a GBP price from the vendor: the Safety Jogger API takes no currency parameter.
- **Currency instability (Safety Jogger).** The same account has returned EUR prices for different products on different runs (a dry run flagged FITZ, FLOWS*, FLUX*, SHARK; a run five hours later flagged FYTS1PSLT, GUSTO81 and others, with no overlap), and one product GET answered "an unexpected response". One response is therefore not proof that a price changed currency.
  - **Re-fetch.** When any cost or RRP of an in-scope product (article type and norm family, `SafetyJoggerScope`, the same rule the mapper uses) comes back non-GBP, or the product GET returns an unexpected response, `ProductFetcher` asks again, at most twice more (a hard cap of 2; `vendorsync.safety_jogger.refetches`). The first re-fetch waits `refetch_delay_seconds` (2, `VENDORSYNC_SJ_REFETCH_DELAY`), the second `refetch_second_delay_seconds` (10, `VENDORSYNC_SJ_REFETCH_DELAY_SECOND`). Both causes share the one budget of 2, every request is throttled like any other, and the first fully GBP response is used. Status errors (4xx, and 5xx and 429 after the client's own retries) are not retried here. An unexpected response that persists is reported as an error `(after 3 attempts)`; the product counts as present and is never discontinued for it.
  - **Held currency flips.** If a product is still non-GBP after the re-fetches, each variant that has a stored GBP price is held: stored cost, price, compare price and availability (`sellable`) are all kept, no price row is deleted, the product is not drafted for it (it stays published if nothing else drafts it), and stock quantity and the other fields from the response are still applied (the 15 minute stock feed keeps working, because availability is not zeroed). The product's payload hash is not advanced, so the next run retries it; the flip is reported every run until the vendor answers in GBP. A variant with no stored GBP price is not held: it is left unpriced and the product drafted `non_gbp_price`, as before.
  - **Reporting.** The run report counts `held_currency` (variants; column `sync_runs.held_currency`) and, in `stats`, `currency_refetches`, `currency_recovered` (products whose re-fetch returned GBP), `response_retries` and `response_recovered`. `vendorsync:catalogue` prints a `Currency:` line and the products still non-GBP. The JSON report file (`sync_runs.report_path`, also written for a dry run) lists every held variant (SKU, product, currency seen, stored cost, price and compare price, `stored_price_written_at`, `offer_updated_at`) and, for every product still non-GBP after its re-fetches, `currency_diagnostics`: per attempt the status, the currency fields seen (a tally of cost and RRP currencies, the non-GBP SKUs with both raw fields, any top-level currency field) and an allow list of RESPONSE headers (date, server, via, age, cache related, request ids, region, country and currency related; a deny list for anything credential-like wins). Request headers and credentials are never stored. Send these to the vendor.
  - **How stale is a hold.** `stored_price_written_at` is the stored price row's own `updated_at`: the writer touches it only when the price or compare price changes, so it says when the GBP values being kept were last written. `offer_updated_at` moves on every run (`last_seen_at`), so it cannot, and is context only.
  - **Alert.** A run that held currency flips is mailed like a run that held prices, with the count and `report_path`. Held flips repeat on every run while the vendor keeps answering in EUR, so this mail repeats too. Once a real mailer is configured (none is today), add a threshold before relying on it if the vendor flaps on most runs: for example mail only above N held variants, or when a variant has been held longer than a set number of days.
  - **Dry run.** `--dry-run` re-fetches exactly like a real run (it only reads) and lists what would be held (`would be kept`), what was recovered and which products are still non-GBP.
  - **Nothing to accept.** `--accept-price-changes` does not apply: there is no GBP value to accept. A hold clears when the vendor next answers in GBP. Products already left unpriced and drafted `non_gbp_price` by the old rule have no last good value; they are priced at the next fully GBP response.
- **Price-change guard.** If a variant's new vendor cost differs from the stored `vendor_offers.cost_price` by more than `VENDORSYNC_PRICE_CHANGE_GUARD_PERCENT` (default 40, exactly 40 passes, 0 switches it off), cost, price and compare price are all kept and the variant is held. The guard looks at cost only, so a markup change never triggers it. It skips creates, variants with no stored cost or price, and products drafted `cost_at_or_above_rrp` or `non_gbp_price` now or at their last write (`no_image` and `vendor_discontinued` do not exempt a product). A held currency flip does not exempt its product: the exemption is judged on the draft reasons after currency holds, so a EUR SKU that is held leaves the 40% check on for the product's other SKUs. A EUR amount never reaches the guard (a non-GBP variant has no cost), and when GBP returns it is compared with the last known good cost, because a hold never touches the stored cost: a real move above 40% is still held for review. A product with a held variant does not advance its stored hash, so every run looks at it again and reports it until it is accepted. The full held list (SKU, product, old and new cost, old and new price, compare prices, percent) is never capped: it goes to a JSON report whose path is in `sync_runs.report_path`, and the count is in `sync_runs.held_prices` and the alert email (a run that held prices is mailed even though it succeeded). Accept with `vendorsync:catalogue SJ --accept-price-changes=SKU1,SKU2` or `--accept-product=ID` (the vendor product ID printed in brackets in reports); a bare `--accept-price-changes` is refused unless `--all` is also passed. `--dry-run` lists what would be held.
- **Repricing existing data.** The stored product hash is `sha256(payload hash | pricing fingerprint)`. The fingerprint is `PricingRules::RULES_VERSION` plus the vendor's markup, so the first run after a rule change or a markup change reprocesses every product once, without `--force`. Bump `RULES_VERSION` whenever a rule change alters stored prices. The currency hold and the re-fetch settings are not part of the fingerprint and did not bump `RULES_VERSION`: for an unchanged payload they leave stored prices exactly as they were, so a bump would only have rewritten the whole catalogue once. A held flip does not advance the product's hash, so it is looked at again on every run until the vendor answers in GBP. After deploying such a change run `vendorsync:catalogue {vendor} --dry-run` first (counts, would-be-held list, non-GBP counts), then the real run, review the held list, then accept what is right.
- **Images** (`vendorsync.images`). Weekly refresh. **No size limit on an original**: every image is decoded, shrunk to at most 1200 pixels wide (`vendorsync.images.max_width`, never enlarged) and stored as WebP (`webp_quality` 85, transparency kept) by `ImageOptimiser`; the vendor's original is not kept. The memory limit is raised for the decode when it is too low and put back afterwards, so a 12 megapixel PNG is stored like any other. `VENDORSYNC_IMAGE_MAX_MEGAPIXELS` and `VENDORSYNC_IMAGE_MAX_FILE_BYTES` default to 0, which is no limit; a value above 0 brings the old rule back (a larger original is replaced by its smaller rendition, or reported). **The server's `.env` must not set `VENDORSYNC_IMAGE_MAX_MEGAPIXELS` to 7 any more**: the environment value wins over the config default. Each stored image carries the custom property `optimised` (`webp-1200-q85`), and the signature is part of the image plan hash, so after this change every product fetches its images again and replaces the ones stored in another format (`php artisan vendorsync:images SJ` and `FS`). **The main images of Safety Jogger and Footsure safety footwear (every category under Safety Footwear) are put on a common baseline** (`vendorsync.images.align`, vendors `['SJ', 'FS']`, collections `['safety-footwear']` (a top level category means all the categories under it), `SubjectAligner`; every other category is left as it comes, and an empty collections list means every category; Footsure's pictures are opaque, so the subject is found by its difference from the background colour): Safety Jogger frames its boots to fill the canvas and its shoes floating in the middle or at the top, which looks misplaced in a grid. The first picture of each colour (role `main`) is shrunk to 1200 px, then (when `vendorsync.images.align.remove_background` is true, the default) the plain background of an opaque photo is made transparent by `BackgroundRemover` (Footsure's shoes on white, so the card backdrop shows behind them): a flood fill from the border through pixels within 24 of the corner colour (largest channel difference), so a white sole inside the shoe stays, and the edge pixels are blended away from the background colour (colour to alpha) so there is no white fringe. It leaves a photo alone when the four corners are not one colour, when under 90% of the border is that colour, or when the fill removes under 5% or over 97% of the picture. Cost: about 2 s for each opaque main image. Then its subject is found (the non-transparent area, or what differs from the background colour of the corners on an opaque image) and moved so the room under it is 15% of the height (`bottom`), scaled down only when it would leave less than 15% above (`top`) or less than 4% of the width at either side (`side`), never scaled up, and kept on its own horizontal centre except that it is never closer than the side margin to an edge. The result is on a square canvas (`aspect` 1.0, as wide as the picture), so a wide shoe and a tall boot share one canvas: the cards fit each picture into a square box and centre it, so without a common canvas shape a wide picture floated in the middle whatever its baseline; an image already on the baseline, or where no subject is found, is stored as it is. The other pictures (details, outsole) are untouched. The stored image's `optimised` property is `webp-1200-q85-base15-top15-side4-ar100-grow-bg`, and the vendor's alignment signature is part of the image plan hash, so switching the rule on or changing `bottom`, `top`, `side`, or `aspect` makes `vendorsync:images SJ` fetch the main images again (and only those). A product with no image stays draft.
  - **Weekly refresh policy (open).** The scheduled `vendorsync:images SJ|FS --refresh` re-downloads every image. For Footsure that is about 24,600 images a week, which is not viable (see Sizing). **Do not enable `VENDORSYNC_SCHEDULE_ENABLED` until the Footsure refresh policy is decided.** Conditional requests (`If-None-Match` or `If-Modified-Since`) are the likely approach. Not implemented.
  - **What gets queued.** Without `--refresh` and `--product`, `vendorsync:images` and the image step of `vendorsync:catalogue` queue a job only for a product whose stored image hash is missing or differs from the hash of its current image plan (changed, incomplete, or cleared before indexing), that has no stored images, or whose plan is blocked (so its job reports the failure). Products with up-to-date images are counted in the run's `skipped_up_to_date` statistic and in the command output. `ImageRun` asks which products have images once per chunk of 200, not once per product; the only per-product read is the stored payload the plan is mapped from, and only for a product that has a hash and images. `--refresh` and `--product` queue everything they name. The job keeps its own up-to-date check, so a change between queuing and running is harmless. The run's `seen` is the number of jobs queued, not the number of products.
  - **Requests.** Up to 3 attempts per URL, 500 ms apart, retried only on connection errors, HTTP 5xx and 429. A 404, 403 or other 4xx fails at once, per candidate URL: an `http` URL is tried over `https` first and then over `http`, so a 404 on it makes two requests, and a 404 on an `https` URL makes one. The 60 s timeout is the total for one request, connect included; the connect timeout is 10 s. An `http` URL is tried over `https` first.
  - **Throttle.** At least 250 ms between the starts of two requests to one host, across all workers. Every HTTP attempt (each retry and each https/http candidate) reserves its own slot under a short lock (5 s), sleeps until the slot, and then requests with no lock held, so a hanging host stalls only its own worker. A worker that cannot get the lock within 5 s fails that image as a lock timeout (`could not reserve a request slot`) and does not try the next candidate.
  - **Concurrency per host equals the number of image workers** (`HORIZON_IMAGES_PROCESSES`, default 2, read by `supervisor-images` in `config/horizon.php`). Only the 250 ms gap is enforced, so raising the Horizon image supervisor count raises the number of concurrent requests to each vendor host.
  - **Queue routing.** Every image job runs on the `images` queue, served only by `supervisor-images` (timeout 960 s, above the job's 900 s; `retry_after` 1200 s above both). The queue names are defined once, in `App\Support\Queues`, and `config/horizon.php` and `config/media-library.php` use those constants. `ImageRun` sends the jobs as a Laravel batch, and **a batch ignores each job's own `onQueue()`**: it pushes with the queue named on the batch, and with none it falls back to the connection default. Until this was fixed every image job landed on `default` (181 waiting there in production while `images` sat idle). `ImageRun` now calls `->onQueue(Queues::IMAGES)` on the batch. Tests: `ImageQueueRoutingTest` checks the `jobs` table for `vendorsync:images` (whole vendor, `--product`, `--refresh`) and the `vendorsync:catalogue` image step; `QueueConfigurationTest` ties job queue, supervisor, supervisor timeout and `retry_after` together, and fails when any queueable class under `app/` does not set its queue from `Queues`. Any new batch must name its queue too. Locally, `queue:work` with no `--queue` only reads `default`: pass `--queue=default,images,conversions` (`composer dev` does).
  - **Sizing (Footsure, measured from the local feed).** `storage/app/vendorsync/footsure/incoming/Productdata_b2b.csv` (27,342 rows) gives 2,787 products (ProductID, Safety Jogger brand excluded), of which 17 list no image and fail their job (`the vendor payload lists no images`). Planned images, counted the way `FootsureMapper` plans them (one per URL and colour): **24,594 in total, 8.8 per product** on average (median 8, p90 16, p99 32, max 71; 3 products have more than 60). Every URL is `http` on one host, `ts-images.gardinerbros.co.uk`, so every worker hits the same host and each download tries `https` first, then `http`: the throttle floor is 24,594 x 250 ms = 1.7 h if the host answers on `https`, and 3.4 h if every image needs both requests. No URL is shared between colours. **The 15 to 60 s per job is an estimate, not a measurement** (to be replaced with Horizon runtimes), and a job with more images takes longer, so a product with 71 images could approach the 840 s deadline (900 s minus the 60 s margin); images not started in time report `not attempted` and the next run completes them. Hours for 2,770 jobs, by image workers and seconds per job:

    | Image workers | 15 s per job | 30 s per job | 60 s per job |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | 1 | 11.5 h | 23.1 h | 46.2 h |
    | 2 | 5.8 h | 11.5 h | 23.1 h |
    | 3 | 3.8 h | 7.7 h | 15.4 h |
    | 4 | 3.4 h (throttle bound) | 5.8 h | 11.5 h |

    The 4 worker, 15 s cell is limited by the throttle, not the workers: 4 workers at 15 s make about 2.4 images per second, which is about 4.7 requests per second when every image needs two requests, above the 4 per second the 250 ms gap allows. Everywhere else the workers are the limit.
  - **Main cost per job: inline conversions.** Conversions run inside the image job (`QUEUE_CONVERSIONS_BY_DEFAULT=false`): each image is converted to its `thumb`, `medium` and `large` renditions with GD before the job moves on, so the CPU and memory of a job are mostly conversion, not download. Follow-up candidate, not implemented: queue the conversions on the `conversions` queue (4 workers, own supervisor), so image workers only download and the two stages overlap. Its cost is that a failed conversion lands in `failed_jobs` instead of the image run's report (docs/launch-checklist.md).
  - **Memory on the 4 GB server (estimates, not measured).** MySQL 1.0 to 1.3 GB, Meilisearch 0.3 to 0.5 GB (more while indexing), Redis 0.1 to 0.2 GB, PHP-FPM about 0.5 GB, OS and Horizon about 0.3 GB: 1.0 to 1.3 GB is left. An image worker holds about 54 MB once booted (measured locally with the console kernel; Horizon and Redis add an estimated 10 to 20 MB). Measured on Windows PHP 8.4 with GD, `memory_limit=256M`, all four conversions inline in one process (`QUEUE_CONVERSIONS_BY_DEFAULT=false`): a 5.0 MP PNG peaks at 99 MB, a 2560 x 2560 PNG (6.55 MP) at 107 MB, a 2560 x 2560 JPEG at 98 MB, and a 7.0 MP PNG of full-range noise (18.7 MB file, near the 20 MB cap) at 147 MB, about 8 MB per megapixel. Each conversion alone peaks within 1 MB of the whole path, so they do not stack. After a job the process holds 55 to 74 MB. `supervisor-images` restarts a worker above 192 MB (`memory`, checked between jobs, not a cap) and after 100 jobs (`maxJobs`). Caveat: Windows PHP bundles GD, so GD buffers count toward `memory_limit`; Debian's `php8.4-gd` may link the system libgd, whose buffers count toward RSS only. Check once on the server: run `php artisan vendorsync:images SJ --product=<an id with a 2560 px image>` (it only queues the job; the Horizon worker decodes the image) and watch the worker with `ps -eo pid,rss,cmd | grep horizon` while it runs. RSS is in KB. The default of 2 workers is the safe choice; set `HORIZON_IMAGES_PROCESSES=3` for a first import only when `free -m` shows at least 1 GB available with everything else running and the server has 2 or more vCPUs, then back to 2. Avoid 4. The CLI `memory_limit` in `php.ini` is the hard limit that kills a worker (`app:preflight` reports it): 256M.
  - **Long-wait alert.** `horizon.waits` for `redis:images` is 10800 s (3 h), so a first import's backlog of thousands of jobs does not fire `LongWaitDetected`.
  - **Time budget.** An image job is killed at its timeout (900 s), so it works to a deadline of that minus 60 s (`job_margin_seconds`). A download starts only when the time left covers the longest it can take: `ImageDownloader::worstCaseDownloadSeconds()`, 197 s for an https URL and 394 s for an http one at the default settings (it grows with `retries`, `timeout`, `retry_backoff_ms` and `slot_lock_seconds`). Otherwise the image fails as `not attempted: job time budget exhausted`, and so does each image after it. A test keeps the budget above the http figure.
  - **First image.** The first image in the vendor's list is the product's hero. While it is missing, the product stays draft with `no_image` even when other images were saved, and the next image run fetches only what is missing and puts the hero first. A product that already has a stored hero (an image with the first image's colour and role, whatever URL it came from) is never held back: if the hero's URL changed and the new one fails to download, the old image stays first and primary, the product keeps its status, and the run says `hero refresh failed, kept existing image` instead.
  - **Reporting.** A job that saves some images but not all succeeds with a warning, in the run's messages and on every run for as long as images are missing: `Product N: PARTIAL, 4 of 6 images saved, 2 failed (K lock timeouts, J not attempted): <each image and its reason>`, or `Product N: HERO IMAGE FAILED, left draft (no_image) until it downloads. ...` when the first image is the one missing. The run stats count `partial_jobs`, `hero_failed_jobs` (products held back), `hero_refresh_failed_jobs` (old hero kept), `images_failed`, `lock_timeouts`, `not_attempted`, `failed_jobs` and `index_failed_jobs` (images saved, indexing failed), and `vendorsync:status` prints them as one line. A run with partial failures finishes `succeeded` and sends no alert. A job that saves nothing fails and lands in `failed_jobs`; a run in which every job failed is `failed` and sends the alert.
  - **Search indexing in the image job.** After the images are saved, `ProductImageSync` sends the product to the search index (it publishes the product when the last draft reason `no_image` goes, and drafts are indexed too). The order is fixed: (1) save media and variant links; (2) clear `vendor_products.image_urls_hash` (and set `images_synced_at`); (3) `refreshStatus()` with search syncing off, so the status save does not index; (4) one explicit `searchable()` call; (5) write the real hash. The hash is therefore only current once indexing succeeded. The clear comes first because anything that stops the job between the status change and the index call (an exception, the job timeout, a killed worker) must leave a hash that does not match, and because on `--refresh` the new hash equals the stored one, so merely not advancing it would change nothing. A product is indexed when its images changed, its hash is new, or it is still draft or flagged `no_image` (so a status change is never skipped). A refresh that changes nothing, on a published product with a current hash, does not index. There is no database transaction in this path (the sync, `refreshStatus()` and the job's `handle()` open none; the run record's transaction comes after), so the call to Meilisearch is never inside one.
  - **When indexing fails.** The images and the status are already in the database. The job fails (`ImageSyncFailed`, so it lands in `failed_jobs`, `errors` +1, `failed_jobs` +1, `index_failed_jobs` +1) with `Image sync failed for vendor product N: X of Y images saved, but search indexing failed (<exception>). The database is up to date and only indexing failed. The image hash was cleared, so the next image run reindexes this product without downloading the stored images again.` Failed images, if any, are listed after it. It is a failure and not a partial result because a partial result succeeds with a warning and sends no alert, which would hide a Meilisearch outage. When every job of a run fails this way the run is `failed` and alerts (the existing rule). **Retry:** the next `vendorsync:images` or `vendorsync:catalogue` image step, or `queue:retry` of the job, finds the cleared hash, reuses the stored images (no downloads), finds the product already published and reindexes it. A product that can never be indexed fails again on every run, with no downloads, until the cause is fixed.
  - **With `SCOUT_QUEUE=true`** (production runs with `false` today) the image job cannot see an indexing failure: `searchable()` only queues a `Laravel\Scout\Jobs\MakeSearchable` job, so the hash is written and the job succeeds. If that queued job later fails, it is a `MakeSearchable` row in `failed_jobs` that does not name the product, and nothing clears the hash. Find it with `php artisan catalogue:verify-index` (products missing from the index, a status or price mismatch in its random sample, failed Meilisearch tasks) and by checking `failed_jobs`; repair with `queue:retry` for that job or `php artisan lunar:search:index`. A Meilisearch task that fails after a successful send does not throw with either setting, so `catalogue:verify-index` is the check in both modes. Tests: `ImageIndexFailureTest`.
  - **Finding partial image sets.** Use `vendorsync:status` and the run messages. `catalogue:verify-media` does not find them: it reports published products with no image or a missing conversion, so a product with 5 of 6 images passes it.
- **Search indexing at the end of a catalogue run.** Every write runs inside `withoutSyncingToSearch()`, so nothing is sent to the search index from inside a database transaction. After the last product, the products the run wrote are sent to the index in chunks of `vendorsync.reindex_chunk` (100). The run is recorded only after that step.
  - **What the report says.** After the counts table: `N products queued for indexing` when Scout queueing is on, or `N products sent to the search index (applied asynchronously; run catalogue:verify-index)` when it is off (also saved as `indexing` in the report file). It never says "reindexed": the run only knows it handed the products over, not that Meilisearch has applied them. Run `catalogue:verify-index` to find out.
  - **A chunk that throws** (for example Meilisearch is down) is reported as an error (`Search indexing failed for N products (Lunar product IDs a to b, chunk i of n): <exception>`) and the next chunk is tried. After **3 failed chunks in a row** the remaining chunks are not attempted.
  - **Payload hashes.** The `payload_hash` of every product in a failed chunk, and of every product not attempted, is set to null. Without that the next run would see an unchanged payload, skip the product and never index it. With it, the next run takes the product through the normal update path and indexes it. A null hash only means "changed": whether a product is created or updated comes from the `vendor_products` row, and the price-change guard compares against the stored `vendor_offers.cost_price` and price, never the hash, so a cost change of more than 40% on a product whose hash was cleared is still held.
  - **Run status.** The run is marked `failed` (so `SyncRunAlert` is sent) and the first message says `Search indexing failed: X of Y written products are not in the search index ... All database writes succeeded; only indexing failed.` A run that already aborted or failed keeps its status and gets the same message added. If the hashes cannot be cleared either, the message says so and tells you to run the vendor again with `--force`.
  - **Not the same as a failed Meilisearch task.** With `SCOUT_QUEUE=false` Scout sends the documents and Meilisearch processes them afterwards: a task that fails there does not throw. This handling only covers errors thrown while sending.
  - **Same pattern elsewhere (not changed).** `StockSync` also persists its database change and then calls `searchable()`. A failure there is not self-healing: stock already matches the feed, so the next run skips the product and never reindexes it. Stock runs mark the run failed. Reindex the affected products by hand, or the whole index with `php artisan lunar:search:index`, to repair them. The image job no longer has this gap (see "Search indexing in the image job" above).
- **Pictograms.** Safety Jogger's `icons` list is written, as it comes, to the product attribute `pictograms` (`name|Label` entries in the vendor's order; its `priority` field is not the display order). `php artisan vendorsync:pictograms SJ` then downloads every listed icon that is not already in `public/images/pictograms/{name}.png`, from `vendorsync.images.pictogram_url` (the vendor's `static/safetyjogger/icon/industrial/{name}.png`, one request each through the throttled downloader, PNG only, never replacing a file). Run it after `vendorsync:catalogue SJ`, and commit new icons if they should ship with the code. Products already imported need `php artisan vendorsync:catalogue SJ --force`, because their payload is unchanged. The storefront shows exactly this list and leaves out an icon whose file is missing. Footsure supplies none.
- **Product sheets.** `php artisan vendorsync:product-sheets SJ [--product=ID] [--refresh]` stores the English product sheets (PDF, one per colour) of Safety Jogger products in each product's media collection `documents` (disk `products`), with custom properties `type` = `product_sheet`, `language` = `en`, `colour` (the colour option value name the images carry), `source_url` and `sha256`. The list comes from the stored payload (`documents` of type `PRODUCT_SHEET`, English only: label `English`, a `_en.pdf` file name, or an `/en/` folder), so no vendor API call is made; the PDFs come from the vendor's public document host through the throttled downloader (a body must start with `%PDF`). A sheet's colour is the code in its file name (`LIGERO_NAV_en.pdf` is the colour with code `NAV`), looked up in the payload's `colors[].code`; a product with one English sheet that names no colour has it for every colour (`colour` unset); any other sheet that cannot be matched is reported, never guessed. A sheet already stored under the same URL is not downloaded again; `--refresh` downloads each again and replaces it only when its content changed; a sheet the vendor no longer lists is deleted once every download succeeded; sheets added by hand (no `source_url`) are never touched. Not scheduled and not queued: it runs in the foreground (about one request per sheet, so a full Safety Jogger run takes a while; run it on the server in a `screen` or `tmux`). Certificates, declarations of conformity and other languages are not mirrored.
- **Small subjects are enlarged.** An aligned image is resampled once from the vendor's full resolution. A subject that a plain resize would leave clearly too small for its room (more than 25% short of filling it: the photographer left a lot of empty space round it) is enlarged to fill the room, never beyond the original's own pixels, so no upscaling blur. Others are only scaled down to fit, as before. The marker ends `-grow`, so only the shoe and boot main images fetch again.
- **Limiting an image run to categories.** `vendorsync:images FS --category=safety-footwear` (contract handles, comma separated; a top level handle means all the categories under it) queues only products in those categories and does not look at the others; `--aligned` means the categories of `vendorsync.images.align.collections`. It applies to a normal run and to `--refresh`; `--product` ignores it. An unknown handle is refused before anything is queued.
- **Commands.** `vendorsync:catalogue`, `vendorsync:stock`, `vendorsync:images`, `vendorsync:status`. `catalogue:restructure-categories` (storefront, not ingestion) is the one-off command that moves a database seeded with an earlier category tree into the current one; see CLAUDE.md, Launch checklist.
- **Progress output.** `vendorsync:catalogue` prints one line per product as the source yields it, in both a real run and `--dry-run`: `{name} ({seen} of {total})`, e.g. `Ligero (23 of 481)`. The name is whatever the source has cheaply (a Safety Jogger list item's `name`, a Footsure row's `Short Product Name`); the count is 1-based against the total the source reported before any detail was fetched. Pure progress: `CatalogueImporter::run()`'s `onProduct` callback never affects what is imported, and nothing else calls it (`vendorsync:stock` and `vendorsync:images` do not).

## Vendor tables (owned by the ingestion module)

Vendor identity and ordering data live here, not in Lunar.

- `vendors`: `id`, `code` (unique, e.g. `SJ`, `FS`), `name`.
- `vendor_products`: `product_id` (Lunar product), `vendor_id`, `vendor_product_id`, `commodity_code` (UK tariff code, may be null). Unique on (`vendor_id`, `vendor_product_id`). Non-unique index on `product_id`. For Safety Jogger `vendor_product_id` is the API product `id`; for Footsure it is the `ProductID` column (each `Family Product Reference` is one colourway of that product).
- `vendor_offers`: `product_variant_id` (Lunar variant), `vendor_id`, `vendor_sku`, `vendor_product_name`, `vendor_colour`, `vendor_size`, `ean` (may be null), `cost_price` (pence ex VAT, may be null), `stock`, `last_seen_at`. Unique on (`vendor_id`, `vendor_sku`). Non-unique index on `product_variant_id`. The `vendor_*` values are the vendor's own wording, used to place orders manually.
- The same Lunar product or variant may be supplied by more than one vendor, so neither table is unique on the Lunar ID. At launch each variant has one offer.
- Cost is never derived from the selling price. It comes only from `vendor_offers.cost_price`.
- The ingestion module adds further tables and columns through new `*_vendorsync_*` migrations: tables `sync_runs`, `vendor_raw_records` and `vendor_field_locks`; columns on `vendor_products` (`payload_hash`, `content_hashes`, `draft_reasons`, `related_ids`, `missed_runs`, `image_urls_hash`, `images_synced_at`) and `vendor_offers` (`sellable`), and on `sync_runs` (`non_gbp_variants`, `non_gbp_products`, `held_prices`, `held_currency`, `report_path`). Deploying the currency change needs `php artisan migrate --force` (it adds `sync_runs.held_currency`). The storefront never depends on them.
