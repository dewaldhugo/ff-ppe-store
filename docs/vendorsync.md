# Vendor ingestion (VendorSync) reference

Scope rules for who may edit what are in CLAUDE.md (Scope boundary, Ingestion phases). This file holds the rules and vendor table definitions.

## Ingestion rules (summary, verified against `config/vendorsync.php`)

- **Safety Jogger.** Scope: `articletype` in `['shoes']` and an EN ISO 20345 or 20347 norm; `excluded_brands` is empty. `zero-protective` only for models `high_boot` and `high` with EN ISO 20345; `low_boot` stays standard-rated until the accountant confirms. Stock buffer 0, and the 15 minute stock feed is the stock authority. SKUs are identified by `skuId`, never by EAN. RRP includes VAT at 20%.
- **Footsure.** Imports everything except brands starting with `Safety Jogger` (case-insensitive). Brand `Miscellaneous Other` maps to no brand. An unmapped `Group Class` drafts the product. Stock buffer 1, stock comes from the daily catalogue run. Placeholder measurements (`999`, `333`, `222`, `111`) are stored as null. A feed file older than 36 hours aborts the run; one dated more than 60 minutes ahead is unreadable.
- **Run safety.** A run that sees fewer than 70% of the previous run's products aborts before writing. A product is drafted after 2 missed runs and never deleted. A run holds its lock for 4 hours. The schedule only registers when `VENDORSYNC_SCHEDULE_ENABLED=true`. Failed, aborted and stale runs mail `VENDORSYNC_ALERT_EMAIL`.
- **Price.** Listing price ex VAT = cost x markup, rounded half up to the penny, with no cap at the RRP. The markup is `VENDORSYNC_MARKUP` (default 1.5), overridable with `VENDORSYNC_SJ_MARKUP` and `VENDORSYNC_FS_MARKUP`; each must be a number from 1.0 to 5 or the run stops when the mapper is resolved. Each mapper has its own `PriceCalculator`. `compare_price` is the RRP ex VAT only when it is above the price. Safety Jogger `rrpPrice` is VAT-inclusive at 20%; Footsure `SRP` is treated as VAT-inclusive at the row's own rate (the feed does not say so: unconfirmed). A missing or zero RRP means no compare price and no check. No cost means the SKU is not imported.
- **Draft reasons for price data.** `cost_at_or_above_rrp`: every variant has a cost at or above its RRP ex VAT (probably a pack cost against a unit RRP). Those variants are held at stock 0, and one such variant among others does not draft the product. `non_gbp_price`: any SKU priced in a currency other than GBP, or with a price but no currency. Such SKUs are imported unpriced (no price row, no cost) with stock 0, never converted, and their product stays draft even when its images arrive. The image pipeline only clears `no_image`; every other stored reason keeps a product draft. Runs report `non_gbp_variants`, `non_gbp_products` and a `non_gbp_currency` tally (`(blank)` apart from an explicit code such as `EUR`), dry runs included. The fix for a `non_gbp_price` product is a GBP price from the vendor: the Safety Jogger API takes no currency parameter.
- **Price-change guard.** If a variant's new vendor cost differs from the stored `vendor_offers.cost_price` by more than `VENDORSYNC_PRICE_CHANGE_GUARD_PERCENT` (default 40, exactly 40 passes, 0 switches it off), cost, price and compare price are all kept and the variant is held. The guard looks at cost only, so a markup change never triggers it. It skips creates, variants with no stored cost or price, and products drafted `cost_at_or_above_rrp` or `non_gbp_price` now or at their last write (`no_image` and `vendor_discontinued` do not exempt a product). A product with a held variant does not advance its stored hash, so every run looks at it again and reports it until it is accepted. The full held list (SKU, product, old and new cost, old and new price, compare prices, percent) is never capped: it goes to a JSON report whose path is in `sync_runs.report_path`, and the count is in `sync_runs.held_prices` and the alert email (a run that held prices is mailed even though it succeeded). Accept with `vendorsync:catalogue SJ --accept-price-changes=SKU1,SKU2` or `--accept-product=ID` (the vendor product ID printed in brackets in reports); a bare `--accept-price-changes` is refused unless `--all` is also passed. `--dry-run` lists what would be held.
- **Repricing existing data.** The stored product hash is `sha256(payload hash | pricing fingerprint)`. The fingerprint is `PricingRules::RULES_VERSION` plus the vendor's markup, so the first run after a rule change or a markup change reprocesses every product once, without `--force`. Bump `RULES_VERSION` whenever a rule change alters stored prices. After deploying such a change run `vendorsync:catalogue {vendor} --dry-run` first (counts, would-be-held list, non-GBP counts), then the real run, review the held list, then accept what is right.
- **Images** (`vendorsync.images`). Weekly refresh. Images above 5 megapixels are replaced by a smaller rendition or reported (GD memory). Files above 20 MB are rejected. A product with no image stays draft.
  - **Requests.** Up to 3 attempts per URL, 500 ms apart, retried only on connection errors, HTTP 5xx and 429. A 404, 403 or other 4xx fails at once. The 60 s timeout is the total for one request, connect included; the connect timeout is 10 s. An `http` URL is tried over `https` first.
  - **Throttle.** At least 250 ms between the starts of two requests to one host, across all workers. Every HTTP attempt (each retry and each https/http candidate) reserves its own slot under a short lock (5 s), sleeps until the slot, and then requests with no lock held, so a hanging host stalls only its own worker. A worker that cannot get the lock within 5 s fails that image as a lock timeout (`could not reserve a request slot`) and does not try the next candidate.
  - **Concurrency per host equals the number of image workers** (2 in production: `maxProcesses` of `supervisor-images` in `config/horizon.php`). Only the 250 ms gap is enforced, so raising the Horizon image supervisor count raises the number of concurrent requests to each vendor host.
  - **Time budget.** An image job is killed at its timeout (900 s), so it works to a deadline of that minus 60 s (`job_margin_seconds`). A download starts only when the time left covers the longest it can take: `ImageDownloader::worstCaseDownloadSeconds()`, 197 s for an https URL and 394 s for an http one at the default settings (it grows with `retries`, `timeout`, `retry_backoff_ms` and `slot_lock_seconds`). Otherwise the image fails as `not attempted: job time budget exhausted`, and so does each image after it. A test keeps the budget above the http figure.
  - **First image.** The first image in the vendor's list is the product's hero. While it is missing, the product stays draft with `no_image` even when other images were saved, and the next image run fetches only what is missing and puts the hero first. A product that already has its hero is not unpublished when another image fails.
  - **Reporting.** A job that saves some images but not all succeeds with a warning, in the run's messages and on every run for as long as images are missing: `Product N: PARTIAL, 4 of 6 images saved, 2 failed (K lock timeouts, J not attempted): <each image and its reason>`, or `Product N: HERO IMAGE FAILED, left draft (no_image) until it downloads. ...` when the first image is the one missing. The run stats count `partial_jobs`, `hero_failed_jobs`, `images_failed`, `lock_timeouts`, `not_attempted` and `failed_jobs`, and `vendorsync:status` prints them as one line. A run with partial failures finishes `succeeded` and sends no alert. A job that saves nothing fails and lands in `failed_jobs`; a run in which every job failed is `failed` and sends the alert.
  - **Finding partial image sets.** Use `vendorsync:status` and the run messages. `catalogue:verify-media` does not find them: it reports published products with no image or a missing conversion, so a product with 5 of 6 images passes it.
- **Commands.** `vendorsync:catalogue`, `vendorsync:stock`, `vendorsync:images`, `vendorsync:status`.

## Vendor tables (owned by the ingestion module)

Vendor identity and ordering data live here, not in Lunar.

- `vendors`: `id`, `code` (unique, e.g. `SJ`, `FS`), `name`.
- `vendor_products`: `product_id` (Lunar product), `vendor_id`, `vendor_product_id`, `commodity_code` (UK tariff code, may be null). Unique on (`vendor_id`, `vendor_product_id`). Non-unique index on `product_id`. For Safety Jogger `vendor_product_id` is the API product `id`; for Footsure it is the `ProductID` column (each `Family Product Reference` is one colourway of that product).
- `vendor_offers`: `product_variant_id` (Lunar variant), `vendor_id`, `vendor_sku`, `vendor_product_name`, `vendor_colour`, `vendor_size`, `ean` (may be null), `cost_price` (pence ex VAT, may be null), `stock`, `last_seen_at`. Unique on (`vendor_id`, `vendor_sku`). Non-unique index on `product_variant_id`. The `vendor_*` values are the vendor's own wording, used to place orders manually.
- The same Lunar product or variant may be supplied by more than one vendor, so neither table is unique on the Lunar ID. At launch each variant has one offer.
- Cost is never derived from the selling price. It comes only from `vendor_offers.cost_price`.
- The ingestion module adds further tables and columns through new `*_vendorsync_*` migrations: tables `sync_runs`, `vendor_raw_records` and `vendor_field_locks`; columns on `vendor_products` (`payload_hash`, `content_hashes`, `draft_reasons`, `related_ids`, `missed_runs`, `image_urls_hash`, `images_synced_at`) and `vendor_offers` (`sellable`), and on `sync_runs` (`non_gbp_variants`, `non_gbp_products`, `held_prices`, `report_path`). The storefront never depends on them.
